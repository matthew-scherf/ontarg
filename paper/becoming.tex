\begin{abstract}
\noindent
An ontological argument derives an existence verdict from a concept alone, a priori. The classical instances begin from the fullest concept --- a maximally perfect being --- and end in a necessary substance; every one of them has been contested at the step from concept to existence, and Kant's diagnosis, that existence is not a real predicate, is still where most readers stop. This paper runs the same ambition from the opposite pole and through the modern instrument that has already been turned on G\"odel's version: a proof assistant. The concept is presuppositionlessness --- a specification that imports no structure, names no constant, distinguishes no element. Four machine-checked theorems then do the work. On the bare distinction exactly one structureless specification has content, $x = \lnot x$ (uniqueness). Read as an equation over states it has no solution (no being). Read as a recursion over time it has exactly one solution per seed, the two-tick clock (unique becoming). And these last two are one fact, not two: a specification has no static solution if and only if every orbit moves at every tick (the frame correspondence). What the emptiest concept forces to exist is therefore not a being --- that is refuted --- but a becoming, and the engine throughout is a single theorem, Lawvere's diagonal at its fixed-point-free instance, read for once as a motor rather than a wall. The step from concept to actuality, which the classical arguments take once and silently, is here taken in public and graded: a conditional grade under four named premises, a tokened grade that any denial refutes in the act of denying, and an enacted grade in which verifying the argument already runs its defining recursion. The core is axiom-free and holds by the Lean kernel; the readings that turn theorems into metaphysics are named, and the seam between them is itself a theorem. What is claimed is not that becoming has been proved to exist without remainder, but that the remainder has been made exact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Anselm's argument is one sentence long. God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived; a being that exists in reality is greater than one that exists in the understanding alone; so if that than which nothing greater can be conceived existed only in the understanding, a greater could be conceived, which is absurd; therefore it exists in reality. The move is entirely a priori. No observation enters, no premise about the world; the existence verdict is squeezed out of the concept by the threat of contradiction.

Almost everyone who has read it has felt that something is wrong, and the history of the argument is the history of trying to say exactly what. Gaunilo, Anselm's first critic, ran the form on a different concept: the most perfect island. By the same reasoning it must exist, since an island that exists is more perfect than one that does not --- and no such island does, so the form proves too much. Aquinas rejected the argument for a different reason: we do not possess the concept of God in the way the inference requires, so the premise about the understanding fails. Descartes revived it, treating existence as one perfection among a perfect being's perfections. And Kant delivered the diagnosis that has held longest: existence is not a real predicate. To say a thing exists adds nothing to its concept; it posits the concept's object. So no analysis of a concept, however rich, can contain the object's existence, and the whole family of arguments commits the same category error at the same joint.

The argument did not die of Kant's objection; it went modal and then went formal. Hartshorne, Malcolm, and Plantinga rebuilt it around necessity: if a maximally great being is even possible, then --- given the right modal logic --- it is necessary, and so actual. G\"odel wrote down an axiomatic version in higher-order modal logic, with positive properties and an essence relation, and left it in a notebook. That version is where the modern instrument enters. Benzm\"uller and Woltzenlogel Paleo put G\"odel's axioms into automated and interactive theorem provers, confirmed the notorious modal collapse the system implies --- that whatever is the case is necessarily the case --- and then found something the pencil-and-paper tradition had missed: G\"odel's original axiom set is outright inconsistent, while a variant due to Scott is not \cite{benzmuller2014,benzmuller2016}. Oppenheimer and Zalta had already used a prover to simplify the argument's logical core \cite{oppenheimerzalta2011}. The lesson of that work is not that the ontological argument succeeds. It is that a proof assistant changes what the debate can be about: not whether the inference feels valid, but whether it is, mechanically, and on which axioms.

This paper takes the same instrument to the same ambition and inverts the starting point. Instead of the fullest concept it begins from the emptiest --- a specification that presupposes nothing, that could be written before any structure is fixed. The question is what, if anything, such a concept forces to exist. The answer, and it is four theorems rather than one intuition, is that the verdict splits. Read one way the concept forces the \emph{non-existence} of any static object answering to it: the necessary being does not merely go unproven here, it is refuted. Read the other way the same concept forces the existence, unique and constructed, of a \emph{process} answering to it. And the two readings are provably one thing. The instrument's verdict, run at this pole, is that the necessary being dies at exactly the point where a necessary becoming is born, and that the point is a single diagonal.

The rest of the paper is one argument in five steps. Step~1: on the bare distinction, exactly one structureless specification has content. Step~2: it has no static instance. Step~3: it has exactly one running instance per seed. Step~4: steps~2 and~3 are the same fact. Step~5: what any of this has to do with actuality, stated in three graded strengths rather than assumed in one. Sections \ref{sec:diagonal}--\ref{sec:modal} isolate the novelty (a fixed-point theorem read as generative) and the modal shape (necessity without a possibility premise); \S\ref{sec:objections} answers seven objections; \S\ref{sec:literature} places the argument; \S\ref{sec:claims} states precisely what is and is not claimed.

\section{Ground Rules}\label{sec:rules}

The argument is carried by theorems, and the theorems are machine-checked. This section says what that buys and what it does not, because the entire force of the paper depends on the reader holding those two apart.

Each cited result is a theorem in Lean~4 \cite{demoura2021}, a proof assistant whose small trusted kernel checks every inference. The files are standalone: prelude only, no imported libraries, no dependencies between files, each checkable with the bare \lf{lean} binary under one pinned toolchain. Each file ends by printing the axioms its results actually use. The reader who wants to verify a claim opens the named file, finds the named theorem, and runs the checker; there is no appeal to authority anywhere in the chain, only to the kernel. The repository accompanying this paper vendors the fifteen files the argument cites. The one file that assembles the argument's core into a single statement, \lf{Becoming.lean}, uses no axioms at all, and its audit prints ``does not depend on any axioms'' for every result.

Every clause of the argument carries one of four verdicts, so its status is never left implicit.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=1pt]
\item[\tagf.] Carried by a machine-checked theorem, cited by file and name.
\item[\tagfr.] Proved undecidable in the relevant vocabulary. What is asserted is the undecidability, not either answer.
\item[\tagp.] A named assumption joining a theorem to an informal reading, listed with what depends on it.
\item[\tagi.] A suggested reading of a theorem, marked and severable. The mathematics stands without it.
\end{description}

This lets the paper state its central methodological claim exactly. The standard it aims at is the standard of G\"odel's incompleteness theorems, and that standard is often misdescribed. It is not that no one can disagree. It is that everyone can see precisely where disagreement is possible, and that it is nowhere in the derivation. The incompleteness theorems are not beyond dispute because they overwhelm objection; they are beyond dispute because the proof is a finite mechanical object and any dispute has to point at a specific step and be wrong. This argument asks for the same treatment. The mathematics of its four core steps is held by the kernel and is where the argument cannot be attacked. The philosophical import --- that these theorems bear on being, becoming, existence --- lives entirely in the readings, and the readings are named, enumerated, and priced. That the seam between theorem and reading cannot be closed by cleverness is itself a theorem: any vocabulary rich enough to express the metaphysical reading of a formal result must draw distinctions the formal vocabulary does not (\lf{Ledger.lean}: \lf{settling\_needs\_new\_primitive}). So the certainty on offer is conditional and delimited. It does not extend to the readings. It covers the derivation completely.

\section{The Argument}\label{sec:argument}

A \emph{specification} is a rule a state is asked to satisfy. It has two natural readings, and keeping them apart is the whole argument. Written $x = f(x)$ it is an equation: a demand for a state equal to its own image under $f$, a fixed point. Written $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$ it is a recursion: a demand for a trajectory, a sequence of states each obtained from the last by $f$. The first reading asks what \emph{is} the case, timelessly; the second asks what \emph{happens}, tick by tick. Call them the object frame and the process frame. For most $f$ both frames are inhabited and nobody notices the distinction. The founding specification of this argument is chosen so that they come apart as sharply as possible.

We build up to it from a constraint on the concept, not from a choice of $f$.

\subsection*{Step 1 --- One candidate}

The concept the argument starts from is presuppositionlessness: a specification that imports no structure. To import no structure is to name no particular element, single out no constant, break no symmetry --- to be sayable before any of the alphabet's elements has been distinguished from any other. On the barest possible alphabet, a single distinction with two sides, this has an exact formal meaning. A law imports no structure when it commutes with the relabeling that swaps the two sides: whatever the law does, it does the same after the sides are exchanged, because it had no way to tell them apart in the first place. This property is called equivariance.

The identification of ``imports no structure'' with ``equivariant'' is not a definition pulled from the air; it is backed by a theorem. On the bare distinction, the laws whose graphs are definable from equality alone --- built up from ``these two are equal'' by negation, conjunction, disjunction, naming nothing --- are exactly the equivariant laws, and no constant law is among them (\lf{Structureless.lean}). Syntactic innocence (nothing is named) and semantic innocence (nothing survives that the relabeling breaks) turn out to be one class.

Given that, the candidates can be counted. On the two-sided distinction there are exactly two equivariant laws: the identity, which sends each side to itself, and the negation, which swaps them. Constants are excluded, since a law that always returns one fixed side is precisely a law that names an element. That leaves two, and one of them says nothing: the identity's equation $x = \mathrm{id}(x)$ is satisfied by every state whatever, so it constrains nothing and generates nothing. The unique structureless specification with any content is $x = \lnot x$.

\begin{prop}[One candidate; \tagf]\label{prop:one}
On the bare distinction, a structureless law is the identity or the negation; a constant law is not structureless; and the identity is satisfied by everything, so it carries no content. The unique structureless specification with content is $x = \lnot x$.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{one\_candidate}, \lf{constants\_not\_structureless}, \lf{id\_says\_nothing}; fuller generality in \lf{OnlyShape.lean}: \lf{spec\_forced}; the equivariance identification in \lf{Structureless.lean}: \lf{structureless\_iff\_equivariant}, \lf{constants\_not\_defble}. Axiom-free.}

What this step does not say. It does not say that ``structureless'' can mean only ``equivariant.'' The theorem narrows the gap between the two --- they coincide for everything definable from the distinction alone --- but a residue remains: the identification of ``importing no structure'' with ``having no vocabulary beyond the distinction itself.'' That residue is the argument's one substantive assumption about the concept, and it is carried as a named premise (R3, \S\ref{sec:claims}). A reader who rejects it rejects the reading of Step~1, and the argument continues below it as a conditional. Nothing downstream is smuggled through this step; the counting is a two-line finite check.

\subsection*{Step 2 --- No being}

Now read the candidate in the object frame, as an equation. It asks for a state equal to its own negation: a side of the distinction that is the other side. There is none. On the two-sided distinction, each side is not the other, so neither solves $x = \lnot x$. At the level of propositions the same wall stands, and in full generality: a proposition equal to its own negation yields a contradiction outright, by the reasoning that drives the liar and Russell's paradox. The presuppositionless candidate, read as a demand for an object, is a demand nothing meets.

\begin{prop}[No being; \tagf]\label{prop:nobeing}
The founding specification has no static instance: $x = \lnot x$ has no solution among states, and a proposition equal to its own negation is absurd.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{no\_static\_instance}, \lf{no\_being}; \lf{TwoReadings.lean}: \lf{object\_reading\_empty}; \lf{Aseity.lean}: \lf{founding\_diagonal}. Axiom-free.}

What this step does not say. It refutes the \emph{presuppositionless} being --- the object answering to the emptiest concept. It says nothing whatever about a structured necessary being, a God defined by a battery of perfections, or any richer object. Those import structure by construction and fall outside the founding specification entirely. The argument is about the ground, the place one reaches by assuming as little as possible, not about theology; the classical arguments' target survives this step untouched, because this step never aims at it.

\subsection*{Step 3 --- Unique becoming}

Read the same candidate in the process frame, as a recursion: $x_{n+1} = \lnot x_n$. Now it is satisfiable, and in the strongest way. Give it any seed, and there is a trajectory --- alternate the two sides forever. There is exactly one such trajectory from each seed, since each step's value is forced by the last. And its shape is fixed: the two-tick clock, $\mathit{true}, \mathit{false}, \mathit{true}, \mathit{false}, \dots$, returning to itself every second tick. Existence here is not asserted; it is constructed. The trajectory is written down (the function \lf{orbit}, and in guarded form \lf{gfix}), and its uniqueness is a short induction. The same object appears once more, and this is worth marking: the G\"odel-style guarded fixed point of the founding specification --- the fixed point built by the discipline that makes self-reference well-founded --- \emph{is} this clock (\lf{godel\_is\_clock}). The construction that in the incompleteness setting yields a sentence asserting its own unprovability yields, here, time.

\begin{prop}[Unique becoming; \tagf]\label{prop:becoming}
Read as a recursion, the founding specification has, from every seed, a solution, exactly one, and that solution is the two-tick clock, of period two.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{becoming\_exists}, \lf{becoming\_unique}, \lf{two\_tick\_clock}; \lf{TwoReadings.lean}: \lf{founding\_two\_readings}; \lf{Loeb.lean}: \lf{deJonghSambin}, \lf{godel\_is\_clock} (within \lf{[propext, Quot.sound]}). Axiom-free.}

What this step does not say. ``Exists'' here means exactly one thing: the recursion has a constructed, unique solution. It does not mean that the clock is instantiated in the world, that anything is actually ticking, or that the process is concrete rather than mathematical. That further question --- actuality --- is Step~5, and it is deliberately held back so that the existence claim of this step can be read at its exact, modest strength: an object exists in the same sense in which the natural numbers exist once the successor function is given.

\subsection*{Step 4 --- The refusal is the running}

Steps~2 and~3 look like two facts about one specification: no fixed point, but a unique orbit. They are one fact. A specification has no static solution if and only if every orbit it generates moves at every tick. Left to right: if some orbit ever held still, the state it held at would be a fixed point, contradicting restlessness. Right to left: a fixed point would be a one-state orbit that never moves. So ``refuses to be'' and ``cannot stop running'' are the same hypothesis on $f$, stated in the two frames, and the equivalence is a biconditional, not a resemblance.

\begin{prop}[The frame correspondence; \tagf]\label{prop:frames}
A specification has no fixed point if and only if every orbit moves at every tick. The object frame's emptiness and the process frame's perpetual motion are one hypothesis read in two frames.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{refusal\_is\_running}; \lf{TwoReadings.lean}: \lf{frames\_one\_fact}. Axiom-free.}

What this step does not say. It is not a metaphor bridging two loosely analogous ideas. It is the load-bearing beam against the charge of equivocation. One might worry that the argument slides between two senses of ``is'' --- ``there is no state that is its negation'' and ``there is a process that runs'' --- and pockets an existence claim in the shuffle. The biconditional forecloses that. The two readings are two formal frames of one specification $f$, and their relationship is a theorem about $f$, proved without passing through natural language at any point. The non-existence of the being and the existence of the becoming are not two verdicts to be reconciled. They are one verdict, seen twice.

\medskip

That is the argument's core. Assembled, the four steps are a single kernel-checked conjunction (\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{ontological\_argument}), axiom-free. In words: from the concept of presuppositionlessness alone, it is a theorem that exactly one specification answers to it, a theorem that nothing static instances it, a theorem that exactly one process does, and a theorem that these last two are the same fact. If an ontological argument is an a priori derivation of an existence verdict from a concept, this is one --- with two inversions. It starts from the emptiest concept, not the fullest. And it ends in a becoming, not a being; the being-version is not left unproven but refuted, on the same specification, in the adjacent frame.

A word on the size of this. That the two-tick clock is mathematically trivial is not a defect of the argument; it is a requirement of it. An ontological argument that leaned on heavy machinery --- rich definitions, choice principles, a detour through the infinite --- would have imported exactly the structure the starting concept forbids, and would forfeit its claim to begin from nothing. The argument is minimal because it must be: any added apparatus would name an element, break the symmetry, and dissolve the emptiness that does the forcing. That the whole argument can be stated in four lines and checked under no axioms is the visible signature of that restraint.

\section{The Running Diagonal}\label{sec:diagonal}

The mathematics of Steps~2 through~4 has one engine, and naming it is the paper's main contribution.

Behind the wall in Step~2 is a theorem of great generality. Lawvere's fixed-point theorem says that if a system can encode its own maps richly enough --- if some carrier point-surjectively enumerates the maps from that carrier to a value type --- then every endomap of the value type has a fixed point. Contrapositively, if you exhibit an endomap with \emph{no} fixed point, no such self-enumeration exists. The endomap with no fixed point is negation: nothing on a two-valued type equals its own negation. Feed that instance into Lawvere and out come, as one argument at different sites, Cantor's theorem (no set surjects onto its power set), Russell's paradox (no set of all non-self-membered sets), Tarski's undefinability of truth, G\"odel's first incompleteness theorem, and the undecidability of the halting problem. The construction is always the same. Build the diagonal map $g(a) = f(e(a)(a))$, ask where it is enumerated, and derive a contradiction at the diagonal. In Lean it is four lines (\lf{OneDiagonal.lean}: \lf{lawvere}, \lf{diagonal\_no\_go}).

The tradition reads all of this one way: as limitation. These are the great negative results, the walls around what sets can contain, what theories can prove, what programs can decide, what languages can define. The diagonal is the instrument of the ``no.''

The finding here is that the same theorem, read in the process frame, is the instrument of a ``yes.'' The fixed point that negation refuses to a state, the recursion grants to a process --- uniquely. \lf{frames\_one\_fact} is the exact hinge: the very restlessness that is the object-frame ``no fixed point'' is the process-frame ``moves at every tick.'' The wall and the motor are one hypothesis. And the identification is not loose: the guarded fixed point built by the standard well-founded discipline, the construction that carries G\"odel's self-referential sentence, is the two-tick clock itself (\lf{godel\_is\_clock}). The diagonal does not only bound being. Run, it generates becoming.

Once seen, the pattern recurs. The same diagonal, at the same fixed-point-free instance, refuses a different totality at each of several sites in the surrounding corpus, and at each site the refusal, read forward, is a positive structural fact rather than a defeat.

Each row is one theorem or a small cluster, and each says the same shape of thing. A system cannot hold a complete model of itself (the certificate), so no system settles from inside whether it is the fundamental ground; the undecidability is a real finding, not a gap. A describer inside the world it describes cannot derive its own anti-diagonal record, so there are facts that are genuinely new --- underivable in principle, not merely uncomputed. A chooser's own prediction of its next choice is falsified by the choice that receives it, so a system can be fully determined from outside and open from within, at exactly the self-referential coordinate. And a total self-doubt is inconsistent --- the doubter cannot coherently doubt its own occurring --- which is the cogito, arrived at as the affirming sign of the same diagonal whose denying sign is the incompleteness wall.

Priority is worth stating precisely, because the neighborhood is old. Spencer-Brown's \emph{Laws of Form} built an algebra on a single mark and read its re-entry --- the mark that contains itself --- as oscillating, generating a rudimentary time; Kauffman and Varela developed that reading into a dynamics \cite{spencerbrown1969,kauffmanvarela1980}. Yanofsky had already shown that the diagonal arguments share one categorical form \cite{yanofsky2003}. Process primacy is older still: Heraclitus, and in the modern era Whitehead \cite{whitehead1929}. What is new here is narrower than any of those and can be said in one sentence. To our knowledge no prior argument derives an existence verdict from the diagonal's refusal itself --- treats the ``no fixed point'' not as a limit to be worked around but as the direct ground of a ``there is a unique process.'' The specific additions carrying that are the uniqueness clause of Step~1 (the candidate is the \emph{only} structureless one with content), the frame-correspondence biconditional of Step~4, and the identification \lf{godel\_is\_clock}. Oscillation was noticed; uniqueness, the biconditional, and the existence verdict were not.

\section{Actuality, Graded}\label{sec:actuality}

The four core steps establish necessity of one specific kind: necessity de dicto, the necessity of a theorem. It is a theorem that nothing static answers to the presuppositionless concept and a theorem that exactly one process does. What none of them establishes is that anything actual answers to the concept --- that some real thing is the ground, that the clock is instantiated, that becoming is not merely a well-defined mathematical object but a running feature of the world. This is the gap every ontological argument must cross, and the classical arguments cross it in one silent stride. Anselm crosses it inside the premise that existence in reality is greater than existence in the understanding. Descartes crosses it by counting existence among the perfections. Plantinga crosses it in the premise that a maximally great being is possible. In each the actuality is already loaded into a value-word --- ``greater,'' ``perfection,'' ``great'' --- and the argument's air of a priori inevitability comes largely from how quietly the loading is done.

The honest thing is not to cross the gap silently but to grade it, and to price each grade. Three grades, in ascending order of what they assume.

\begin{prop}[Grade 1 --- conditional actuality; \tagf{} given R1--R4]\label{prop:regress}
If anything occurs, and grounding demands are answered (R1), well-founded (R2), with unseeded grounds structureless (R3) and generative (R4), then every chain of grounding demands terminates in a ground whose law is $x = \lnot x$.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Regress.lean}: \lf{time\_selects\_the\_shape}; the four premises are the named hypotheses \lf{answered}, \lf{wf}, \lf{structureless}, \lf{generative} in the signature. Within \lf{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}.}

This grade buys actuality on credit. If the world has grounds, if grounding does not regress forever, if the bottom imports no structure and does something, then the bottom is the founding shape. The four ``if''s are named premises, each with a single dependent --- this proposition and nothing else --- and each stated as a hypothesis in the theorem, not as background. A reader who denies any of them keeps the entire core of \S\ref{sec:argument}; only this bridge to the actual world falls.

\begin{prop}[Grade 2 --- tokened actuality; \tagf]\label{prop:cogito}
The denial of any token's occurrence refutes itself in the act of denial: to token ``this does not occur,'' the token must be given, which is its occurring. Occurrence needs no bearer and cannot be coherently denied from where it occurs.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{denial\_self\_refutes}; \lf{Cogito.lean}: \lf{no\_self\_denial}, \lf{cogito\_valid\_necessarily}. Axiom-free.}

This grade needs no premise at all, only a tokening, which the denier supplies by objecting. It is the cogito, split from the classical version along a line the classical version blurred. What is necessary is the \emph{validity} of the seat's self-affirmation: for any token, its occurrence is certain and its denial self-defeating, provable with no instance in hand. What is \emph{not} thereby established is that any particular carrier is inhabited --- some types are empty, so ``there is a doubter'' is contingent, not a theorem (\lf{Cogito.lean}: \lf{existence\_is\_contingent}). The cap is part of the result: occurrence is affirmed, a bearer of it is not.

\begin{prop}[Grade 3 --- enacted actuality; \tagf, the reflexive reading \tagi]\label{prop:instantiation}
The class of runs of a known law from a known seed has width one: whoever holds the law and the seed holds a single world. Over such a generated class nothing is free and every proposition is expressible in every vocabulary --- the map provides a complete theory of its territory, and the territory, instantiated by a concrete run of the theory, is indistinguishable from the map with respect to every expressible distinction.
\end{prop}

\dem{\lf{Instantiation.lean}: \lf{run\_class\_generated}, \lf{map\_is\_territory}. \lf{run\_class\_generated} within \lf{[Quot.sound]}; the conjunction within \lf{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}.}

This grade is the strangest and the strongest, and its extra strength is an interpretation, flagged as such. The Lean kernel does not merely inspect the recursions it verifies; it normalizes them --- it runs them. Every time \lf{TwoReadings.lean} or \lf{Becoming.lean} is checked, the two-tick clock is computed, the founding recursion evaluated tick by tick inside the act of verification. On the reading that a concrete execution of the defining recursion --- in a proof assistant, or in any other physical medium --- counts as a genuine occurrence of the process, then verifying the core theorems already instantiates the clock: the kernel does not merely describe it but enacts it, in the only sense the law itself can distinguish --- a run that realizes every step the law prescribes and no step it does not. The claim is emphatically not that the kernel's micro-physics mirrors the clock's shape; the hardware is transistors, the software a normalizer of terms. It is that inside the run class every distinction expressible in the law's own vocabulary is forced to a definite value and no further distinction can be drawn (\lf{map\_is\_territory}), so the description and the run are indistinguishable to every reading the law affords --- the corpus being, in this sense, a generated territory rather than a map of one, its law the source files and its run the kernel's check. That formal completeness is forced. Whether it amounts to the metaphysical identity of map and territory --- whether, in the limit case of a world generated wholly by its own law, the description simply is the thing described --- depends on what one takes such identity to require, and the argument adopts that reading while marking it, here, as the interpretation it is.

The grades do not collapse into each other and must not be collapsed in summary. Grade~1 is a conditional on four premises. Grade~2 is unconditional but reaches only to occurrence, not to a bearer. Grade~3 is unconditional and concrete but leans on a reading of what a computation is. What the argument does not have, at any grade, is a premise-free proof that becoming is actual without remainder. What it has instead is a ladder, each rung labelled with its price, where the classical arguments have a single unlabelled step.

\section{Necessity Without a Possibility Premise}\label{sec:modal}

The modal ontological arguments turn on a specific engine. From ``it is possible that a maximally great being exists'' they reach ``a maximally great being exists,'' and the reaching needs two things: that great-making is such that a merely possible great being would be necessary, and that the modal logic is S5, so that possible necessity collapses to plain truth. The whole weight sits on the possibility premise. Grant that a maximally great being is possible and, in S5, the rest follows; deny it and nothing does. Critics have long observed that the possibility premise is very nearly the conclusion --- that to grant the possibility of a necessary being is most of the way to granting the being.

This argument has no possibility premise, because it needs none. Its necessity is not the box of a modal logic applied to a contingent-looking claim; it is the necessity of a theorem, true across every admissible world because it is true of the structures themselves. And the modal logic it lives in is not assumed to be S5 in order to make a detachment go through. S5 is a \emph{result}. Reading necessity as truth at every admissible world and possibility as truth at some, the corpus proves that the verdict calculus validates K, T, and the characteristic collapse of iterated modality, and that necessity and possibility are duals --- the full S5 package --- with no accessibility relation anywhere in the vocabulary for a weaker logic to live on (\lf{Modality.lean}: \lf{modality\_is\_S5}). There is nothing to grant. The modality is fixed by the setup, and Steps~1--4 are necessary in the only sense the setup allows: they hold at every world of every admissible class, because they are facts about $x = \lnot x$, which every such world shares.

Two contrasts sharpen the point. First, against the modal arguments: where they must assume S5 and feed it a possibility premise, here S5 is proved and no possibility premise appears --- the existence verdict of Step~3 is a construction, not a detachment from possible necessity. Second, against G\"odel's version specifically. G\"odel's system implies modal collapse: in it, whatever is the case is necessarily the case, contingency vanishes, and this is widely regarded as a defect the mechanization made undeniable \cite{benzmuller2014}. The calculus here does not collapse. Contingency survives as a proved third verdict --- a proposition can be \emph{free}, true at some admissible worlds and false at others, and the trichotomy necessary/impossible/contingent is itself a theorem (\lf{Modality.lean}: \lf{verdict\_trichotomy}). The argument gets its necessity for the core without paying G\"odel's price of flattening everything else into necessity too. What is forced is forced; what is free stays free; and the difference is decidable.

\section{Objections and Replies}\label{sec:objections}

\paragraph{1. Equivocation.} \emph{The argument trades on two senses of ``is'': there is no state that is its own negation, but there is a process that runs. The existence claim is pocketed in the slide from one to the other.}

There is no slide, because there is no natural-language bridge to slide across. The two ``senses'' are two formal frames of one specification $f$ --- the object frame $x = f(x)$ and the process frame $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$ --- and the relation between them is a theorem about $f$, proved in Lean without any sentence of English carrying an inference (\lf{frames\_one\_fact}). The non-existence of the fixed point and the existence of the orbit are not two claims the prose reconciles. They are one biconditional. If the objection holds, it holds against the theorem, and it can be checked there.

\paragraph{2. Triviality.} \emph{This is a flip-flop. $x = \lnot x$ oscillates; of course it has no fixed point and a periodic orbit. Dressing that up as an ontological argument adds nothing.}

The minimality here is forced, not incidental (\S\ref{sec:argument}): heavier machinery would import the very structure the starting concept forbids. What remains of the objection is whether smallness means shallowness, and it does not. The mathematics is deliberately small and fully exposed; that is a feature the argument shares with the result it takes as its standard, since the diagonal core of the incompleteness theorems is also a few lines. Smallness is not triviality. The force is in the conjunction: that the oscillating law is the \emph{unique} structureless law with content (Step~1), that its object frame is not merely hard but provably empty (Step~2), that its process frame is not merely inhabited but uniquely so and constructively (Step~3), and that these are one fact (Step~4) --- each axiom-free, each checked. And the obvious escape, that a richer alphabet might dodge the oscillation and buy back a static solution, is closed: on any alphabet whose structureless relabeling reaches every value, the escape fails, and naming a value that blocks it is importing structure (\lf{RichAlphabet.lean}: \lf{no\_rich\_separation}, \lf{escape\_closed}). A small checked argument that survives the escapes beats a deep contested one.

\paragraph{3. The load is in R3.} \emph{``Structureless equals equivariant'' is where the work happens. Define structurelessness that way and the conclusion is baked in.}

The identification is not stipulated; it is a theorem for everything definable from the distinction alone, and no constant survives it (\lf{structureless\_iff\_equivariant}, \lf{constants\_not\_defble}, both axiom-free). What remains after that theorem is a small residue: the reading of ``imports no structure'' as ``has no vocabulary beyond the distinction.'' That residue is named as premise R3 and it is Step~1's only load. Deny it and the argument does not collapse; it becomes conditional on your preferred sense of structurelessness, and Steps~2--4 run unchanged over whatever candidate that sense selects, provided the candidate is restless. The premise is a joint, labelled and isolated, not a hidden hinge.

\paragraph{4. Kant.} \emph{Existence is not a real predicate. Whatever this argument is doing, it cannot be deriving existence from a concept, because that is the one thing concepts cannot yield.}

The argument obeys Kant rather than defying him. Existence never appears as a predicate in it. The object-frame verdict is a \emph{refutation} --- a demonstration that a certain equation has no solution --- and no solution is asserted to have the property of existing. The process-frame verdict is a \emph{construction} --- a trajectory is exhibited and shown unique --- and this is exactly the mode in which Kant allows existence to be established: not by unfolding a concept but by exhibiting an object. The natural numbers exist, in the only sense mathematics needs, because the successor function gives them; the clock exists in that same sense and no stronger. Kant's target was the inference from a concept's content to its object's reality. Step~3 makes no such inference. It builds the object.

\paragraph{5. Gaunilo.} \emph{Run the parody. Take the concept of a perfect island and derive its existence by the same form. If the form is valid it proves too much.}

The parody is blocked by a theorem, and this is a genuine advantage of the formal version over Anselm's. Anselm's argument fell to Gaunilo because nothing in it individuated its concept: ``that than which nothing greater can be conceived'' could be swapped for ``island'' and the machinery ground on. Step~1 is a uniqueness clause. It does not say the founding specification is a nice candidate among others; it says it is the \emph{only} structureless one with content, and that every rival --- island, or anything else with individuating features --- imports structure, which is a checkable property (\lf{constants\_not\_structureless}; the definability characterization in \lf{Structureless.lean}). A perfect island names an element; naming an element is importing structure; imported structure disqualifies the candidate before Step~2 is reached. The concept here is individuated by classification, so there is no room to substitute.

\paragraph{6. No actuality.} \emph{Nothing here shows that anything actually runs. The clock is a mathematical object; the world is not obliged to contain it.}

Correct, at grade~0, and the grading is the reply rather than a dodge. The argument does not claim premise-free actuality, and \S\ref{sec:actuality} states exactly what each stronger grade buys and what it costs: four named premises for the conditional grade, a tokening for the grade that reaches occurrence, a reading of the kernel's own computation for the enacted grade. The classical arguments took this same step, and took it tacitly, inside a value-word. The difference is not that this argument crosses the gap for free. It is that it shows the reader the gap, and the toll on each bridge.

\paragraph{7. Why call it ``ontological.''} \emph{Whatever its merits, this is not an ontological argument. The term has a settled meaning, and inverting the concept and the conclusion abandons it.}

The definitional criterion of an ontological argument is that it derives an existence verdict a priori, from a concept alone, without empirical premises. This argument meets it: the concept is presuppositionlessness, the derivation is four theorems, no observation enters. What it inverts are not the criteria but the traditional \emph{choices} made within them --- the emptiest concept in place of the fullest, a becoming in place of a being, a graded actuality in place of a smuggled one. Those inversions are the finding, not a departure from the genre. If anything they return the genre to its cleanest form, since the one place the classical arguments strain hardest against Kant --- treating existence as content to be unpacked --- is exactly the place this one does not go.

\section{Relation to the Literature}\label{sec:literature}

The argument sits in two lineages, and its debt to each is different.

\paragraph{The ontological lineage.} From Anselm's \emph{Proslogion} \cite{anselm} through Gaunilo's reply \cite{gaunilo}, Aquinas's rejection \cite{aquinas}, Descartes' restatement \cite{descartes1641}, and Kant's diagnosis \cite{kant1781}, the tradition argued from a maximally perfect being toward a necessary substance. Hegel stands apart in this lineage and closest to the present argument in content; he gets a paragraph below. The modal reconstruction runs through Hartshorne \cite{hartshorne1962}, Malcolm \cite{malcolm1960}, and Plantinga \cite{plantinga1974}, and its formal apex is G\"odel's axiomatic version \cite{godel1995}, scrutinized by Sobel \cite{sobel1987} and then mechanized by Benzm\"uller and Woltzenlogel Paleo, who confirmed the modal collapse and exposed the inconsistency of G\"odel's axioms \cite{benzmuller2014,benzmuller2016}; Oppenheimer and Zalta's prover-assisted simplification \cite{oppenheimerzalta2011} belongs to the same computational turn, and Oppy's survey maps the whole terrain \cite{oppy1995}. This argument's relation to the lineage is inversion, not refutation. It does not claim the classical arguments fail; it changes the starting concept and the target verdict and reports what a proof assistant says at the other pole. Where the mechanized G\"odel work checked an argument \emph{for} a conclusion its author sought, this work's core includes a refutation --- of the presuppositionless being --- carried by the same instrument, and treats a graded, priced actuality as the honest form of the step every prior argument took at a bound.

\paragraph{Hegel.} The nearest precedent in content is the opening of Hegel's \emph{Wissenschaft der Logik} \cite{hegel}. Pure being, wholly indeterminate, having no property to distinguish it from anything, is indistinguishable from nothing; being passes into nothing and nothing into being; and the truth of the pair is not either but their movement, becoming. That is, in narrative form, the present argument's spine: begin from the most indeterminate concept, find that it yields no stable being, and find that what it does yield is passage. The difference is the whole point of doing it this way. Hegel asserts the passage; he narrates being's collapse into nothing and their resolution in becoming as a dialectical movement the reader is asked to follow and accept. Here the assertion is replaced by two theorems and a biconditional: indeterminacy, made precise as structurelessness, provably yields no being-instance (Step~2) and provably yields a unique becoming-instance (Step~3), and the passage between the two verdicts is not a dialectical movement but \lf{frames\_one\_fact}, an equivalence the kernel checks. What Hegel narrated, the kernel verifies. And what the kernel cannot verify --- that our world is an instance of this movement --- is not narrated over but named, as the premises and readings of \S\ref{sec:actuality}. The gain over Hegel is not that assumptions disappear, but that they become visible. What the dialectic merges --- the movement of thought and the movement of being --- the present argument divides into a checked derivation and a priced residue, with the seam between them itself a theorem.

\paragraph{The process and formal lineage.} On the side of content, process primacy is ancient --- Heraclitus's flux --- and modern in Whitehead's making process and creativity ultimate \cite{whitehead1929}. On the side of the mark and its re-entry, Spencer-Brown \cite{spencerbrown1969} and Kauffman and Varela \cite{kauffmanvarela1980} read self-containing distinction as generating oscillation and time. The formal engine is Lawvere's fixed-point theorem \cite{lawvere1969}, whose reach across the diagonal arguments Yanofsky unified \cite{yanofsky2003}. The split cogito descends from the performative reading of Descartes' inference \cite{hintikka1962}. This argument's addition to that lineage is stated narrowly in \S\ref{sec:diagonal}: not process primacy, not oscillating re-entry, but the derivation of an existence verdict from the diagonal's refusal, with the uniqueness clause, the biconditional, and the \lf{godel\_is\_clock} identification as the load-bearing new parts.

\section{What Is Claimed, and What Is Not}\label{sec:claims}

\paragraph{Claimed.} The five steps at their tagged strengths. Steps~1--4 (\S\ref{sec:argument}) as \tagf, axiom-free, assembled in \lf{Becoming.lean}: \lf{ontological\_argument} --- uniqueness of the candidate, non-existence of the static instance, unique construction of the process, and the equivalence of the last two. The graded actuality of \S\ref{sec:actuality}: grade~1 \tagf{} given R1--R4, grade~2 \tagf{} and premise-free but capped at occurrence, grade~3 \tagf{} in its mathematics with the reflexive reading marked \tagi. The novelty of \S\ref{sec:diagonal} at exactly the strength worded there.

\paragraph{Not claimed.} This argument does not show that God exists or does not exist; Step~2 refutes only the presuppositionless being, and a structured necessary being lies outside its scope entirely. It does not show that becoming is actual without remainder; it grades and prices that step and leaves the toll visible. It does not claim novelty for process primacy, for oscillating re-entry, or for machine-checking an ontological argument. It does not claim that the small mathematics is deep. It claims that the mathematics is checked, the readings are named, and the ratio between them is exact and public.

\paragraph{The premise accounting.} The core (grade~0, Steps~1--4) depends on no premise: its mathematics is complete in \lf{Becoming.lean} with an empty axiom set. Above the core, the premises are these.

\paragraph{Refutation versus rejection.} The two are different, and the difference is the argument's whole shape. To \emph{refute} the core is to find a kernel error: a second structureless law with content, a static solution of $x = \lnot x$, a second orbit from some seed, or a failure of the biconditional. Each is a definite mathematical event, and each is where the argument \emph{cannot} be attacked, because the kernel has checked its absence. To \emph{reject} the argument is to deny a named premise or a marked reading --- R3's residue, or one of R1--R4, or the reading of the kernel's check as an instantiation. Rejection is available at every one of those joints, and each joint's row states exactly what survives it: deny R1--R4 and keep all of Steps~1--4; deny R3's residue and keep the whole argument as a conditional; deny grade~3's reading and keep grades~0 through~2. There is no move that is both cheap and total. That is the sense in which the argument aims at the incompleteness standard: not immunity from disagreement, but disagreement forced to name its price.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The classical ontological argument starts from the fullest concept and reaches for a necessary being, and it has spent nine centuries defending the step from concept to existence against the charge that concepts do not contain their objects. This argument starts from the emptiest concept and reaches a different verdict, in two frames of one specification. As an object, the presuppositionless candidate is refused: $x = \lnot x$ has no static solution, and the refusal is a theorem. As a process, it is granted, uniquely and by construction: the same specification, read as a recursion, is the two-tick clock. And the refusal and the grant are one fact, the object frame's ``no fixed point'' and the process frame's ``moves at every tick'' proved equivalent. What the emptiest concept forces into existence is a becoming, and the being-version is not left open but ruled out beside it.

The step to actuality is not smuggled. It is a ladder: conditional on four named premises, or reaching to bare occurrence with no premise at all, or resting on the reading that the kernel, in checking this argument, has run its clock. Each rung is labelled. And the engine under all of it is one theorem read against its tradition --- the diagonal that walls being off from every totality is, set running, the thing that makes becoming. The fixed point negation refuses to a state, it grants to a process; the wall, turned, is the motor.
